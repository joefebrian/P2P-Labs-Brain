# Skit

> Part of [[00 UGC Playbook – Index]] · Choose styles with [[04 Style Selection Guide]] · Hooks: [[02 Hook Library]] · CTAs: [[03 CTA & Offer Library]] · Generate: [[05 AIOSCreator Script Generator Spec]]

## What it is
Scripted scenes with two or more characters (often one creator playing both) that open mid-conflict. The product is revealed as the 'secret', ending on a punchline and a character-voiced offer.

## When to use it
Use it for entertainment-first awareness, relatable workplace or relationship dynamics, and products with a 'what's your secret?' angle. It is the natural fit for the ShortDrama and Characters modules.

## Beat map
| # | Beat | Timing | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **cold open** | 0–3s | A dialogue line mid-scene ('What brings you in today?') |
| 2 | **conflict** | 3–15s | Character B is skeptical, curious or annoyed |
| 3 | **reveal** | 15–25s | The product as the secret |
| 4 | **banter/benefits** | 25–40s | Benefits delivered as natural dialogue |
| 5 | **punchline + offer** | last 8s | Twist + offer said in character |

## Hook formulas
- `What brings you in today? (doctor/nurse skit)`
- `Why do you look so good in every {situation}?`
- `Babe, I love you, but can I tell you something?`
- `Hey, what's that in your bag?`
- `{N} meetings, {N} projects, dude. (workplace)`
- `My battery's about to die so I'm gonna make this quick.`
- `Hey, if you {use product}, you'll get {absurd reward}… (disclaimer parody)`

## Script structure as observed
conflict/question in line 1 -> character B is skeptical/curious -> product revealed as the "secret" -> quick benefit exchange (natural dialogue, not a pitch) -> punchline/twist -> offer mentioned by a character -> CTA. Median ~48s; long doctor/nurse skits 85-110s.

## CTA patterns observed
- "They have a money back guarantee, but you probably won't use it." (Shapermint 4/11/13)
- "She said just don't be silly and pay $45 when you can get it for a huge discount. 40% off with a bunch of free..." (Everyday Dose 18)
- "That stuff's a cheat code. And it flies out, so grab it before the sale's gone." (Dr. Squatch 22)
- "Look, if she can find something she likes, so can you, so head to Oats Overnight" (Oats 30)

## What the strongest examples do
- It opens mid-conversation; no intros.
- Benefits are dialogue, not a pitch; the skeptic character voices the viewer's doubts.
- The offer is delivered in character with a joke ('money back guarantee, but you probably won't use it').

## Visual / production spec
**Observed (keyframes reviewed):** two-person scenes (same creator playing both roles or two actors), doorway/hallway/kitchen/doctor's office, caption headline naming the situation ("Have you got the tickets?", "Why do you look so good in every outfit?", "POV you need Testosterone Support"), a fake press conference (Gymshark 23 "OFFICIAL PRESS CONFERENCE"), a phone Do-Not-Disturb/text-message screen skit (Oats 3), a FaceTime-style call (Oats 35), gym/running b-roll. Some use "DISCLAIMER" parody text.

**Spec:** 9:16. Two-shot or shot/reverse-shot with a costume or angle switch; caption header naming the scene; natural audio. Median about 48 s; doctor skits run 85–110 s.

**Categories seen:** mushroom coffee, shapewear, food, soap/men's grooming, sleep app (Rise), SaaS (Motion AI assistant skits 6/26/29, Canva 25), earplugs, antiperspirant (Carpe). Ideal for ShortDrama module.
